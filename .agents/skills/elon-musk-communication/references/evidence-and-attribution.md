# Evidence and attribution

This repository is **unofficial** and makes two different kinds of claims:

- **Reported practices**: statements attributable to identifiable former executives or published accounts.
- **Maintainer synthesis**: operational templates, review checklist, escalation decision tree, guardrails, and quality scoring created for this skill.

## Documented accounts

1. **Jon McNeill, The Knowledge Project (Nov 10, 2026)** — episode description discusses three-sentence problems and early delivery of bad news. A public episode listing with chapter at about 09:32: https://share.snipd.com/episode/68c8219b-e2e7-4de3-a101-dbcd5c629315
2. **Jon McNeill, My First Million (2026), interview transcript around 39:30** — recalls experimenting with three-sentence emails framed as **problem → analysis of root cause → proposed solution**: https://ulisten.ai/channels/my-first-million/ex-tesla-president-reveals-everything-elon-does-to-win_GG4TwQEYdBY/transcript
3. **Reporting on Musk's 2018 Tesla employee communication memo** — advocating shortest-path communication and reduced jargon: https://www.inc.com/justin-bariso/elon-musk-just-sent-an-extraordinary-email-to-tesla-employees.html

## Read with caution

- McNeill's comments are **first-person recollections**, not a universal, verified policy binding every Tesla employee at every time.
- The accounts describe at least two framing variants: **problem / root cause / solution** and **problem / proposed solution / next step**. This project offers both.
- "Bad news travels fast" is a principle summarized from interviews; don't claim it is a verbatim, independently verified Musk quote.
- This repository's alert template, decision tags, owner/deadline rubric, privacy safeguards and automation are original operational adaptations.
- A reported preference for shorter paths **does not** remove legal, organizational, safety, or confidentiality requirements.
- Re-check cited third-party reporting before presenting claims as historical facts; links can change.
